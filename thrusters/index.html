Electric propulsion in space: the electric thrusters that have flown

Each spacecraft (or series, or constellation) that has flown an electric thruster, placed on its orbit around an animated Earth: gridded ion engines, Hall thrusters, electrothermal (resistojet, arcjet), electrospray and FEEP, pulsed (PTFE pulsed plasma, vacuum arc, pulsed MPD) and other plasma thrusters (HEMPT, RF, hollow cathode, quad confinement). Pulsed thrusters are drawn firing discrete pulses. Air-breathing electric propulsion has not flown (as of November 2026). Thruster models and key parameters

About the data

Compiled from agency, manufacturer and peer-reviewed sources (IEPC, AIAA and journal papers), listed on every entry. Constellations are shown as one entry tagged cluster; families of near-identical satellites as one entry tagged series. Positions: spacecraft still in orbit are at their real current position, propagated with SGP4 (satellite.js) from CelesTrak orbital elements refreshed daily; retired spacecraft fly their catalogued orbit (CelesTrak SATCAT inclination, perigee, apogee; for decayed low orbits the final pre-decay altitude is replaced by 450 km); spacecraft without a catalogue match use a representative orbit for their class. Constellations show a sample of 240 of their real satellites. Deep-space trajectories: JPL Horizons. Sun and Moon positions and Earth's rotation are computed for the displayed time. The Overview scale compresses distances (log-like); the True scale buttons show real distances. Images: Earth day map NASA Goddard (Blue Marble; R. Stöckli, R. Simmon) via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0; city lights NASA Earth Observatory (Black Marble 2016), public domain; clouds and Moon maps Solar System Scope, CC BY 4.0. Please report any error.

Views and models. Earth's shadow uses the true geometry (cylindrical umbra, real Sun direction). The firing schedule is an illustration of typical practice, not mission telemetry: no firing in eclipse (solar-electric power), GEO north–south station keeping in two windows a day centred on right ascension 90° and 270°, GOCE firing continuously (drag-free control), other spacecraft firing in sunlight; pulsed thrusters always pulse (at the published flight pulse rate where one exists). The ground track is drawn from the same orbit model as the position. Official NASA 3D models (NASA 3D Resources, github.com/nasa/NASA-3D-Resources) are used for Dawn, Deep Space 1, EO-1, the SSL 1300 platform and DART (DART simplified from NASA's 3D-print file); the thruster drawn on them is ours and its position approximate. Spacecraft shapes for GOCE, Starlink, SMART-1, Hayabusa2, Psyche, BepiColombo and LISA Pathfinder are simplified, with proportions taken from public images (each is labelled in the view). DART close-up: trajectory from JPL Horizons (DART −135, Didymos, Dimorphos 120065803), drawn as a straight final approach along the Horizons direction; derived impact 23:14:24.18 UTC (NASA: 23:14:24 UTC), 6.14 km/s; body sizes from Daly et al. 2023 (Nature 616, 443); the spacecraft break-up and the ejecta are illustrations. The inset shows the real DRACO images of the final phase from the NASA Planetary Data System (Small Bodies Node, DART bundle v2.0; every third frame, all frames in the last 15 s; NASA/Johns Hopkins APL) at their recorded times, and “NASA replay video” plays NASA’s replay of the final 5.5 minutes (hosted copy, reduced to 640 px). Live contact: for Psyche, BepiColombo, Hayabusa2 and the ended DSN-tracked EP missions, the page reads NASA's public DSN Now feed (eyes.nasa.gov/dsn) every 30 s and shows when a NASA Deep Space Network antenna is talking to the spacecraft (📡 in the list), with data rate, band, distance and light time; ESA and JAXA ground stations are not in that feed. Charts: thruster parameters as in the table; a range is drawn as a bar with the point at its upper end.
