Supersonic gas-curtain IPM: Elmer field + Geant4 Monte Carlo

An independent re-simulation of a published beam-profile monitor, built from open tools and compared with the original CST results.

The study

The supersonic gas-curtain ionisation profile monitor (SGC-IPM) images a proton beam without touching it: the beam crosses a thin, 45°-tilted sheet of argon, ionises a tiny fraction of it, and an electrode stack pulls the Ar⁺ ions onto a micro-channel plate (MCP), whose image is the beam profile. Its extraction system was designed in CST and published by Parambil et al. (Instruments 10 (2026) 4). I rebuilt it from the paper alone, with open-source codes, to check how well an independent model reproduces their predictions and to add the physics a field-and-tracking study leaves out.

  1. GeometryRepeller, ten field rings and the MCP, with positions and both voltage sets taken from the paper.
  2. Field (ElmerFEM)Axisymmetric electrostatic solution on a gmsh mesh, exported as a 0.1 mm field map of the whole chamber.
  3. Monte Carlo (Geant4 11.2)A real proton beam crosses the curtain; ionisation, delta electrons and ion birth come from Geant4's EM physics (option 4 + PAI). Ions and electrons are tracked through the Elmer field in Geant4.
  4. Gas motionEach ion starts with the jet velocity and thermal spread; background ions start at rest-gas thermal speeds.
  5. Background and lightResidual-gas N₂⁺ ions from the whole beam path, and gas-light photons emitted and transported to the MCP.
  6. Detector and statisticsMCP efficiency vs impact energy, 25 µm binning, Gaussian fits; bootstrap, Poisson and four-seed errors on every number.

Gas densities are raised 10⁷× inside Geant4 so enough collisions happen to give good statistics; every count is weighted back by 10⁻⁷ (the density is still far too low for multiple collisions). Electron collisions are switched off after birth for the same reason. The background pressure, the MCP efficiency curve and the gas-light yields are estimates, not measurements of this chamber. This re-simulation is my own follow-up to the paper and is not part of the published work.
