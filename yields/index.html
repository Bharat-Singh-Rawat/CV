Secondary electron & sputtering yields

Two surface processes every beam-line, ion-source and accelerator vacuum system has to live with: how many electrons a surface emits per incident electron or ion, and how many atoms an ion knocks out of it. Compiled from published measurements and the standard empirical formula, for many beams, energies and materials.

Electron-induced secondary electron yield δ(E)

Secondary electron yields as tabulated in D. C. Joy's Database of Electron–Solid Interactions (SE data sets published 1931–2003), each data set plotted separately with its original reference. Most sets give the true secondary yield δ (electrons emitted below ~50 eV; some early work used 70 or 100 eV), but some are total yields that include backscattered electrons, e.g. Hilleret et al. (CERN, 2000); check the original reference before comparing sets. The scatter between data sets for the same metal is large (it can exceed a factor of 2) and probably reflects surface condition (adsorbed gas, oxide, carbon), the emitted-energy window and the measurement technique; Joy also notes that the quality of much of the data is poor. For insulators, charging can bias values strongly: Joy advises treating insulator yields with caution unless the provenance of the original data is well documented.

Source: D. C. Joy, A Database of Electron–Solid Interactions, revision 04-02 (2004), Section 1; first described in D. C. Joy, Scanning 17, 270 (1995). Values copied as published, without corrections; data sets marked ⚠ (dotted line) contain an apparent typo or a known caveat in the source, explained in the hover text.

Ion-induced electron yield γ(E)

Electrons emitted per incident ion from clean metal surfaces (evaporated in situ in ultra-high vacuum, 5×10⁻¹¹–5×10⁻¹⁰ Torr) at normal incidence; total uncertainty ±4 % (as stated by the authors). The table values were interpolated by the authors between their measured points. For He⁺ a substantial part of the yield is potential emission. Values for gas-covered (technical) surfaces can differ considerably. The paper's Table I also lists H₂⁺ yields (per atom), not shown here.

Sputtering yield Y(E) at normal incidence

Solid lines: the Yamamura–Tawara empirical formula (eq. 15 of the reference) with the best-fit target parameters Us, Q, W and s of its Table 1, evaluated for the chosen ion. The parameters were fitted by the authors to the measured yields published up to 1994; individual measurements scatter around the curve (compare with the points). Points: measured yields digitised from the figures of the same compilation (glyph centres, digitised twice independently and reconciled; reading uncertainty about ±10 % in E and Y; ambiguous or overlapping symbols omitted); the original papers are listed in the legend of each cited figure.

The formula is a fit, not a measurement: near the threshold energy the yield falls steeply and small parameter changes move it a lot; where a measured set is shown, compare with the points. Yields for compounds, oblique incidence, rough or hot surfaces differ.

Formula and parameters: Y. Yamamura and H. Tawara, Energy dependence of ion-induced sputtering yields from monatomic solids at normal incidence, NIFS-DATA-23 (National Institute for Fusion Science, 1995); published as At. Data Nucl. Data Tables 62, 149 (1996). Note: eq. (17) of the NIFS report prints the two α* branch conditions interchanged; the assignment used here (0.249(M2/M1)0.56 + 0.0035(M2/M1)1.5 for M1 ≤ M2) reproduces the report's own worked example (1 keV He → Au, Y = 0.140 with the report's masses) and the report's α* plot (Fig. 1). Two entries of Table 1 are corrected here: Mo uses W = 0.45 Us = 3.07 (the value in every Mo figure caption and plotted curve; the table's 2.39 repeats vanadium's value), and U has Z = 92 (the table prints 93).
